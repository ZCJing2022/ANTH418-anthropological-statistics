ANTH418 Assignment 8: Roman-Period Iron-Smelting Geochemistry

Research question

Does mean CLR-Si differ across HCM, MAS, SIL, and JUR in the teaching dataset, and how robust is the conclusion to imperfect ANOVA assumptions?

Case study

Żabiński, Grzegorz, Jarosław Gramacki, Artur Gramacki, Ivan S. Stepanov, and Marcin Woźniak. 2023. Old Wine into New Wineskins? “Legacy Data” in Research on Roman Period East Germanic Iron Smelting. PLOS ONE 18(10): e0289771. Read the case study.

HCM is the Holy Cross Mountains, MAS is Masovia, and SIL is Silesia. JUR is the Kraków–Częstochowa Jura comparator, which includes late-medieval slag and modern ore from different contexts than the main study groups.

The observational unit is one legacy chemical analysis of a slag or ore sample. Multiple observations can come from the same site or locality, so they may be more alike than observations from different sites. The teaching file has no site identifiers to assess that dependence.

The teaching file contains 240 observations and the fixed teaching variable Si_CLR.

element_value is a centered-log-ratio (CLR) transformed Si value, not a raw Si concentration. It is the log of Si divided by the geometric mean of Si, Ca, Al, and Mn for that observation, using the source study’s imputed composition.

2. Check assumptions

Assess:

  • distribution shape and possible outliers;
  • normality by region;
  • variance homogeneity.

Use the distribution plots and group SDs alongside formal diagnostics. Use a Q–Q plot for each group to assess normality. Consider the small Jura sample when interpreting its diagnostics, and explain how you treated any potential outliers.

All three omnibus tests assume independent observations. Discuss how site-level clustering could affect the results.

# Your code

For normality, use Shapiro–Wilk by region.

For variance homogeneity, use a Levene-style mean-centered absolute-deviation test as the primary diagnostic. Use the absolute distance from each value to its group’s mean as the response for this diagnostic. You may also report Bartlett’s test as a supplementary check, keeping in mind its sensitivity to departures from normality.

Do not interpret a non-significant assumption test as proof that an assumption is true.

5. Sensitivity checks

Compare the classical ANOVA conclusion with:

  • Welch one-way ANOVA, which also tests equal means and allows unequal variances. It still assumes independence and relies on approximate normality for its reference distribution, especially with small groups.
  • Kruskal–Wallis, which compares groups using ranks without requiring normality. With similarly shaped group distributions, a difference in ranks can be interpreted as a shift in location.
# Your code

7. Interpretation

Separate:

  • the omnibus ANOVA result;
  • eta-squared;
  • Welch and Kruskal–Wallis sensitivity checks;
  • Tukey pairwise comparisons;
  • the assumption caveat.

Interpret regional differences as differences in relative compositional balance, not simply as differences in raw Si concentration.

Final interpretation

  1. What is the unit of analysis, and what does CLR-Si represent?
  2. What does the classical ANOVA indicate about regional differences?
