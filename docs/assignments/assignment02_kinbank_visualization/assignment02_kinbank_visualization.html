NoteBefore you begin

Read the Week 5 Study Guide and the Assignment 2 instructions before starting. This assignment is limited to exploratory data analysis and visualization. The goal is to learn what the data show, identify questions worth investigating, and recognize what the visualizations alone cannot establish.

Case Study

Kinbank is a global database of kinship terminology designed for systematic cross-cultural comparison (Passmore et al., 2023). The ANTH418 teaching dataset is a frozen later snapshot containing 1,287 languages represented by at least one Kinbank form record. Each row represents one language.

The variable kin_term_count is the number of Kinbank form records linked to a language in this teaching snapshot. It is not automatically the number of unique kin concepts and should not be treated as a direct measure of kinship-system complexity.

Research question

What can exploratory visualization reveal about the structure of the Kinbank teaching sample and variation in kin_term_count, and what dependencies or limitations must be considered before moving from description to inference?

5. Consider non-independence

In a short paragraph, explain how phylogenetic autocorrelation, spatial autocorrelation, or both could affect interpretation of the patterns you visualized.

Related languages may resemble one another because of shared ancestry and inherited terminology. Geographically close languages may resemble one another because of contact, borrowing, regional history, or shared environments.

You are not required to calculate autocorrelation, construct a phylogenetic tree, or fit a phylogenetic model. Visualization can make dependence visible; it does not correct for dependence.

6. Final EDA interpretation

Conclude by answering all three questions:

  1. What do your visualizations show about the observed Kinbank teaching sample?
  2. What is one question or apparent pattern worth investigating further?
  3. What is one conclusion these visualizations alone do not justify?

Use descriptive language such as in this sample, the distribution shows, the graph suggests, and worth investigating. Do not claim that the graphs prove a universal cross-cultural pattern, causal relationship, or evolutionary process.

ImportantStatistical scope

No hypothesis tests, p-values, confidence intervals, causal analyses, phylogenetic comparative models, or spatial autocorrelation tests are required.
