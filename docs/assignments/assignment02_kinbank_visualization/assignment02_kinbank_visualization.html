ANTH418 Assignment 2: Visualizing Kinship Diversity with Kinbank

NoteBefore you begin

Read the Week 5 Study Guide before starting this assignment.

The Study Guide explains the required concepts, visualization strategy, and R workflow for exploratory data analysis (EDA), categorical and numerical variables, bar charts, histograms, boxplots, faceting, visual comparison, and the distinction between description and inference.

Research question

How does the number of Kinbank form records linked to languages vary across the teaching dataset, and what do different visualizations reveal about that variation across language families and macroareas?

Case Study

Kinship terminology—the words people use to classify and refer to relatives—varies considerably across human societies. Kinbank is a global comparative database designed to make this variation available for systematic cross-linguistic comparison (Passmore et al. 2023). Rather than treating English kin categories as universal, Kinbank uses a standardized etic genealogical framework onto which language-specific kin terms can be mapped.

The ANTH418 teaching file contains 1,287 languages represented by at least one Kinbank form record in the frozen snapshot used for this course. Each row represents one language. The variable kin_term_count records the number of Kinbank form records linked to that language. The file also contains language-family and macroarea metadata, together with latitude and longitude where available.

kin_term_count requires careful interpretation. It is a count of form records in the database, not automatically the number of unique kin concepts and not a direct measure of the linguistic complexity of a kinship system. Missing family, macroarea, and coordinate values are retained as source metadata gaps.

Assignment

Using the provided Kinbank teaching dataset, conduct an exploratory data analysis (EDA) of variation in kin_term_count. The objective is to use data visualization to discover, compare, and communicate patterns in a comparative anthropological dataset.

Begin by inspecting the dataset and identifying its unit of analysis, variables, measurement scales, and missing values. Then use appropriate bar charts, histograms, boxplots, and faceted plots to examine how represented languages are distributed across broad categories and how kin_term_count varies within the dataset and across relevant groups.

Use the visualizations as analytical tools rather than illustrations added after the analysis. Compare alternative views of the same data and consider what each visualization makes visible, what it may conceal, and how graphical choices affect interpretation.

Your interpretation should maintain a clear distinction between description and explanation. A visible difference among language families or macroareas does not by itself explain why that difference exists. Languages may also share historical relationships and therefore should not automatically be treated as completely independent cultural observations (Passmore and Jordan 2020). Your task is to establish what the teaching data show before considering why those patterns may exist.

Remember that kin_term_count is the number of Kinbank form records linked to a language in this frozen snapshot. A language with a larger value should not automatically be described as having a “more complex” kinship system.

Also remember that languages can share historical ancestry. Similarities among related languages may therefore reflect shared history rather than independent cultural responses. This problem of non-independence is important for cross-cultural research, but you are not required to conduct phylogenetic analysis in this assignment.

Final interpretation guidance

  5. What anthropological claim do these exploratory data alone not support?
  6. Why should apparent differences among language families or macroareas be interpreted cautiously?
ImportantStatistical scope

This assignment is limited to exploratory data analysis and visualization. No inferential statistical tests, phylogenetic models, PCA, or causal analyses are required.
