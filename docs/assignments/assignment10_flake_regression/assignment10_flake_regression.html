ANTH418 Assignment 10: Predicting Flake Mass

Research question

How well does mean flake thickness predict log-transformed flake mass, and what do the fitted line and residuals reveal about the model?

Case study

Bustos-Pérez, Guillermo, and Javier Baena. 2021. Predicting Flake Mass: A View from Machine Learning. Lithic Technology 46(2):130–142. Read the case study.

The teaching dataset contains 300 experimental flint flakes from the published study.

library(tidyverse)

data_path <- if (file.exists("data/flake_regression.csv")) {
  "data/flake_regression.csv"
} else {
  "assignments/assignment10_flake_regression/data/flake_regression.csv"
}

dat <- read_csv(data_path, show_col_types = FALSE) |>
  mutate(
    log_weight = log10(weight)

1. Inspect and visualize

The unit of analysis is one experimental flake. The predictor is mean thickness in millimetres. The response is the base-10 logarithm of flake mass measured in grams.

Summarize weight, mean_thickness, and log_weight.

Then make a scatterplot of mean_thickness versus log_weight with a fitted linear regression line and a 95% confidence band for the mean log response. Label the axes with the predictor units and the response scale.

# Your code

Before fitting the model, describe:

  • the direction and form of the relationship;
  • whether the relationship appears approximately linear on the log-mass scale;
  • whether there are obvious unusual observations.

2. Fit the simple linear regression

Fit the model:

log10(weight) ~ mean_thickness

# Your code

The model assumes an approximately linear mean response on the log scale, independent errors, and roughly constant residual spread. Approximate normality of errors supports the usual model-based intervals. Flakes produced under shared experimental conditions may have related errors. The teaching file has no grouping identifiers to assess this.

Report:

  • the fitted intercept;
  • the fitted slope;
  • the 95% confidence interval for the slope;
  • the p-value for the slope;
  • R-squared.

3. Interpret the slope on the original mass scale

The slope is the fitted change in log₁₀ mass per millimetre of mean thickness.

For a one-millimetre increase in mean thickness, calculate:

# If b1 is your fitted slope:
# 10^b1
# (10^b1 - 1) * 100

Interpret:

  • the multiplicative change in fitted geometric-mean mass;
  • the approximate percentage change in fitted geometric-mean mass.

4. Examine residuals

Create a residual-versus-fitted plot.

Also inspect a normal Q–Q plot of the residuals. To discuss influence, inspect the standard regression residuals-versus-leverage plot with Cook’s distance contours.

# Your code

Discuss whether the residuals show:

  • obvious curvature;
  • strongly changing spread;
  • large residuals, unusual thickness values (high leverage), or points that can materially change the fitted line;
  • major departures from an approximately normal residual distribution.

Use the diagnostic plots to judge how well the simple model represents these data. Investigate unusual points and explain their relevance. An unusual point alone is insufficient reason to exclude a flake.

5. Interpretation and limits of prediction

Interpret the intercept carefully.

The observed mean-thickness range is 1.8–20.2 mm, so an intercept corresponding to 0 mm thickness is outside the observed data range and should not receive a physical interpretation.

Explain:

  • what R-squared tells you about the variation in log10(weight) explained by mean thickness;
  • what the residuals reveal about prediction error on the fitted data;
  • why the confidence band for the mean log response is narrower than a prediction interval for one comparable new flake at the same thickness;
  • why in-sample R-squared and residuals describe fit to these flakes and cannot establish predictive accuracy on unseen assemblages;
  • why a statistically strong relationship does not mean that mean thickness alone determines flake mass;
  • why the fitted trend may be unreliable outside the observed 1.8–20.2 mm range;
  • why results from this experimental assemblage should not automatically be generalized to every archaeological lithic assemblage.

Final interpretation

Answer the following questions in complete sentences:

  1. What is the unit of analysis?
  2. What are the estimated slope, its 95% confidence interval, and R-squared?
  3. What does the slope mean on the original flake-mass scale?
  4. What do the residuals and the experimental data-generating process imply about the limits of prediction and archaeological generalization?
