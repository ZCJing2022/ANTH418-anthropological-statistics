ANTH418 Assignment 4: Sampling Archaeological Radiocarbon Data

Author

Your Name

Case Study

The p3k14c project assembled a large global database of archaeological radiocarbon determinations for comparative research while also documenting important differences in data quality and sampling coverage (Bird et al., 2022). For this exercise, you will use a frozen Southern California subset of 984 radiocarbon determinations derived from p3k14c. For teaching purposes, this complete file will be treated as the reference population from which samples can be drawn.

Assignment

Using the provided radiocarbon dataset, investigate how random sampling, sample size, and sampling bias affect estimates of the mean conventional radiocarbon age. The objective is to apply the Week 7 concepts of population and sample, parameter and statistic, sampling variability, sampling distributions, standard error, and representativeness to an archaeological dataset.

First, use repeated random sampling to examine the sampling distribution of the sample mean at different sample sizes. Compare the center and spread of these distributions and explain how increasing sample size affects sampling variability and standard error. Then compare random sampling with a deliberately restricted sampling frame. Determine whether increasing the number of observations from this restricted frame removes the difference between its estimates and the reference population.

Your interpretation should distinguish clearly between sampling variability and sampling bias. Larger random samples generally produce more precise estimates, but a large sample drawn from an unrepresentative frame can remain systematically biased. For this exercise, treat conventional radiocarbon age as a numerical variable for studying sampling behavior; radiocarbon calibration, summed probability distributions, and paleodemographic inference are not required.

5. Interpretation

In concise prose, explain:

  1. What is the unit of analysis, and what are the population parameter and sample statistic examined here?
  2. What happens to sampling variability and standard error as sample size increases?
  3. How does the restricted sampling frame demonstrate the difference between sampling variability and sampling bias?
  4. Why can a large sample be precise but unrepresentative?
  5. How does this exercise relate to Bird et al. (2022) and the problem of sampling filters in archaeological radiocarbon databases?
  6. What archaeological claim does this exercise not support?

Keep the scope of your conclusion appropriate. This exercise demonstrates properties of sampling using a Southern California teaching subset. It does not reconstruct prehistoric demography or evaluate dates-as-data methods generally.

Submission

Submit both of the following files:

  2. Rendered HTML file (.html)

Your report should contain the required summaries, sampling distributions, restricted-frame comparison, visualizations, and concise interpretation. Do not conduct confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, radiocarbon calibration, summed probability distributions, or paleodemographic modelling.
