How to use this workbook

Week 8 established the general logic of statistical inference. Week 9 applies that logic to comparing two groups.

research design → inspect the groups → estimate the difference → estimate SE → calculate t → quantify uncertainty → assess magnitude → diagnose assumptions → choose alternatives if needed → archaeological interpretation

Do not begin with t.test(). Begin with the research question, the observational unit, the distributions, and the size and direction of the difference.

Learning objectives

By the end, you should be able to distinguish independent and paired designs; calculate and interpret a mean difference; explain the standard error of a difference; interpret the t statistic and degrees of freedom; explain why Welch’s t-test is the independent-samples default; inspect assumptions and robustness; interpret a t-based confidence interval; distinguish raw from standardized effect magnitude; calculate Cohen’s d and Hedges’ g; explain rank-based and permutation alternatives; and apply the workflow to Tell Tweini isotope evidence.

Part II — The effect must have a direction

2. Define Group 1 − Group 2

For independent groups:

\[ \widehat{\Delta}=\bar X_1-\bar X_2 \]

For paired observations:

\[ D_i=X_{1i}-X_{2i}, \qquad \bar D=\frac{\sum D_i}{N} \]

mba_mean <- independent_demo |>
  filter(phase == "MBA") |>
  summarise(m = mean(delta13c)) |>
  pull(m)

lba_mean <- independent_demo |>
  filter(phase == "LBA") |>
  summarise(m = mean(delta13c)) |>
  pull(m)

mba_minus_lba <- mba_mean - lba_mean

Part III — Standard error belongs to the difference

3. Sampling uncertainty for two means

Welch:

\[ SE_{\text{Welch}}=\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^2}{n_2}} \]

Paired:

\[ SE_{\bar D}=\frac{s_D}{\sqrt{N}} \]

mba_demo <- independent_demo$delta13c[independent_demo$phase == "MBA"]
lba_demo <- independent_demo$delta13c[independent_demo$phase == "LBA"]

welch_se <- sqrt(
  var(mba_demo) / length(mba_demo) +
    var(lba_demo) / length(lba_demo)
)
welch_se
[1] 0.1568439

Part V — Degrees of freedom shape the reference distribution

5. Different designs use different df

Student independent: \(df=n_1+n_2-2\)

Paired: \(df=N_{\text{pairs}}-1\)

Welch uses the Welch–Satterthwaite approximation and can return fractional df.

Part IX — Assumptions and robustness

9. Four distinct issues

  • Scale: the response should support meaningful means.
  • Independence: determined by design and archaeological context.
  • Distribution shape: severe skew and influential outliers matter especially at small n.
  • Variance structure: Student assumes equal variances; Welch relaxes this.
Important

Independence is not a visual property of a histogram and cannot be rescued by a large sample. Repeated measurements, fragments from one original object, or observations clustered within one context may contain less independent information than the row count suggests.

Part XI — Raw and standardized effect magnitude

11. Original units remain primary

n1 <- length(mba_demo)
n2 <- length(lba_demo)

sp <- sqrt(((n1 - 1) * s1^2 + (n2 - 1) * s2^2) / (n1 + n2 - 2))
cohen_d <- (mean(mba_demo) - mean(lba_demo)) / sp

WarningDo not turn diagnostics into a pass/fail ritual

With n = 10 per group, formal normality diagnostics have limited power. A Shapiro–Wilk p-value cannot establish independence and should not mechanically determine whether you are “allowed” to use a t-test.

Part XX — Effect magnitude

20. Raw difference and Cohen’s d

n_mba <- length(mba)
n_lba <- length(lba)
sd_mba <- sd(mba)
sd_lba <- sd(lba)
pooled_sd <- sqrt(((n_mba - 1) * sd_mba^2 + (n_lba - 1) * sd_lba^2) / (n_mba + n_lba - 2))
cohen_d <- raw_diff / pooled_sd
raw_diff
[1] 1.089915
cohen_d
[1] 1.179392

Cohen’s d expresses the observed difference in pooled within-group standard-deviation units.

Optional extension — Hedges’ g

df_pooled <- n_mba + n_lba - 2

hedges_g <- J * cohen_d

Part XXII — Alternatives and archaeological interpretation

22. Changing the procedure can change the question

If severe skew, influential extreme observations, or other features made a mean-based t-test difficult to justify, a commonly used rank-based alternative for two independent groups is the Mann–Whitney/Wilcoxon rank-sum test. It should not simply be called “the t-test for non-normal data.”

Practice 22A

Why is “Shapiro–Wilk p < .05 → automatically use Mann–Whitney” too mechanical? What should be considered in addition to a normality diagnostic?

Part XXIII — Conceptual debugging

23. Diagnose the mistake

  1. “Two groups means independent samples.”
  2. “Equal sample sizes mean the design is paired.”
  3. “A large t means the raw effect must be large.”
  4. “Degrees of freedom are just a reporting convention.”
  5. “Welch is only for data that fail a variance test.”
  6. “A nonsignificant variance test proves equal variances.”
  7. “If p < .05, the effect is archaeologically important.”
  8. “Cohen’s d is always more meaningful than the raw isotope difference.”
  9. “A valid t-test proves the observations were independent.”
  10. “A significant isotope difference establishes its causal mechanism.”
